A good housewarming gift says "welcome home" and then quietly earns its place on a shelf, in a drawer or on the kitchen counter. These housewarming gift ideas are sorted by type and budget, so you can pick something useful for a first apartment, a family house or a friend who just moved across the country.

## What makes a great housewarming gift

New homeowners and renters are juggling boxes, paint samples and a long list of things they forgot to buy. The best housewarming gifts help with that list instead of adding clutter. Keep three rules in mind:

- **Useful beats decorative.** Unless you know their taste well, a beautiful object they use every day wins over a statue they have to find room for.
- **Consumable is safe.** Good food, candles, coffee and soap get enjoyed and leave no trace, which is perfect when you are unsure.
- **Neutral is kind.** If you go for decor, pick simple colors and natural materials that fit almost any room.

## Kitchen gifts for the new place

The kitchen is usually the first room people set up and the one where gaps show fastest. A few ideas that rarely miss:

- **A quality chef's knife or a knife sharpener**, because the knives that survived the move are often dull or mismatched.
- **A large wooden cutting board**, which doubles as a serving board for the first dinner party.
- **A cast-iron or enameled pot**, a gift that lasts for decades and makes soups, stews and bread.
- **A set of glass storage containers**, so leftovers and pantry staples finally have a home.
- **A nice olive oil and spice starter set**, since an empty pantry is one of the most annoying parts of moving.
- **A coffee or tea station gift** such as a pour-over set or a teapot with good loose-leaf tea; see our guide to [gifts for coffee and tea lovers](blog:coffee-and-tea-gifts) for more.

## Plants and greenery

A plant makes a bare room feel lived-in instantly. Choose wisely, though: not everyone has a green thumb, and some plants are toxic to cats and dogs, so check before you give one to pet owners.

- **Low-maintenance houseplants** like snake plants, pothos or ZZ plants, which forgive missed waterings and low light.
- **A herb kit for the windowsill**, so basil and mint are always within reach of the stove.
- **A stylish planter or plant stand** if they already own plants that survived the move in a plastic pot.
- **Seeds or bulbs with a garden voucher** for people who just got their first yard.

## Decor that fits any style

Decor is the trickiest category because taste is personal. Stick to pieces that are easy to like and easy to move:

- **A soft throw blanket** in a neutral tone, which works on any sofa and makes the new place feel cozy right away.
- **Scented candles or a diffuser**, a classic for good reason, especially in a fresh, gentle scent.
- **A custom map or print of their new neighborhood**, a personal touch that marks the start of a new chapter.
- **Picture frames** with a photo from their old place or of friends who helped with the move.
- **A doormat with a simple design**, the first thing guests see and a gift almost every new home lacks.

## Practical starter kits

Some of the most appreciated housewarming gifts are the least glamorous. Build a small kit in a basket or a nice storage box:

- **A basic toolkit** with a hammer, screwdrivers, a tape measure and picture hooks, because every move ends with shelves to hang.
- **A first-aid kit and a fire extinguisher**, unexciting but genuinely caring gifts for a new homeowner.
- **A cleaning kit** with good cloths, a few eco-friendly cleaners and a dish brush that looks nice by the sink.
- **A "first night" box** with toilet paper, light bulbs, batteries, snacks and a bottle of something to celebrate.
- **Extension cords and power strips**, since outlets are never where you need them.

## Housewarming gifts by budget

You do not need to spend much to make a good impression. Here is a rough guide:

- **Under $25:** a candle, a herb kit, a set of nice dish towels, a bottle of good olive oil or a small plant.
- **$25 to $50:** a cutting board, a throw blanket, a planter with a plant, glass storage containers or a stocked toolkit.
- **$50 to $100:** a cast-iron pot, a quality knife, a personalized print, a set of wine glasses or a coffee maker.
- **Over $100:** a group gift such as a small piece of furniture, a robot vacuum or a good set of cookware.

For a group of friends or colleagues, pooling money for one bigger gift from a shared wish list is often more welcome than five small ones.
