Finding gifts for dad is famously hard, mostly because dads tend to say "I don't need anything" and then buy the thing themselves the week before. The fix is to stop shopping for "a dad" and start shopping for *your* dad: the griller, the fixer, the hiker or the guy who has opinions about coffee beans.

## Gifts for the grill master

If your dad claims the barbecue every summer, lean into it. Grill gifts get used often and come with bragging rights.

- **A digital meat thermometer**, so he can stop cutting into the steak to check it; it is the upgrade most grillers swear by once they have one.
- **A quality set of long-handled grill tools** in a storage case, replacing the mismatched tongs he has had for years.
- **A rub and sauce collection** from different regions, which turns the next cookout into a tasting.
- **A cast-iron griddle or pizza stone for the grill** that expands what he can cook outdoors.
- **A personalized apron or cutting board** with his name or a family joke, for a touch of fun.

## Gifts for the handyman dad

Some dads see a squeaky door as an invitation. For the fixer, think quality and organization.

- **A compact multi-tool** he can keep in his pocket or glove box, the kind of thing he will use far more often than expected.
- **A good headlamp or work light**, which frees both hands for jobs under the sink or in the attic.
- **A tool organizer or wall storage system** that finally tames the garage.
- **A precision screwdriver set** for electronics, glasses and small repairs.
- **A woodworking or DIY workshop** if he likes to learn new skills.

## Gifts for the outdoor dad

For the dad who is happiest on a trail, at a lake or by a campfire, practical gear wins.

- **An insulated water bottle or thermos** that keeps coffee hot through a whole morning of fishing.
- **A comfortable daypack** with plenty of pockets for hikes and day trips.
- **A camping chair or hammock** for relaxing at the campsite or in the backyard.
- **Merino wool hiking socks**, a small luxury that makes long walks much more comfortable.
- **A field guide to local birds, trees or stars**, adding something new to every outing.

## Gifts for the tech dad

If he reads product reviews for fun, a smart gadget will make his day. Ask casually what he has been eyeing, or pick an accessory that complements what he already owns.

- **Noise-canceling headphones** for commutes, flights or a quiet hour in a busy house.
- **A wireless charging station** that charges his phone, watch and earbuds in one spot.
- **A smart home starter item**, like a smart plug or bulb, if he likes to tinker.
- **A digital photo frame** loaded with family photos, which mixes tech and sentiment.

- **A burr coffee grinder**, the single biggest step up for anyone still using pre-ground coffee.
- **A pour-over set or French press** if he wants to try a new brewing method.

- **A large insulated mug** that keeps coffee hot through his first few emails.

## Gifts for the sports and fitness dad

Whether he plays, trains or mostly cheers from the couch, sports gifts are easy wins.

- **Gear for his sport**, such as golf balls, a new tennis grip, cycling gloves or a quality running belt.
- **A foam roller or massage gun** for recovery after a long run or a weekend of yard work.
- **Merchandise from his favorite team**, like a jersey, cap or framed print.
- **Tickets to a game** you can attend together, often the gift he will remember longest.

## FAQ

### What do you get a dad who says he wants nothing?

Give him an experience or time together, like a round of golf, a fishing trip or a dinner out with the family. You can also upgrade something he uses every day, such as his wallet, coffee mug or work gloves; he will notice the difference without feeling you spent on "stuff."

### What is a good last-minute gift for dad?

Experience vouchers and gift cards for his favorite hobby shop can be bought instantly. Pair one with a handwritten note about a favorite memory, and it becomes a personal gift rather than a fallback.

### How much should I spend on a gift for dad?

There is no right number, and a $20 gift that matches his hobby beats an expensive one he never uses. As a rough guide, under $25 buys grill rubs, a multi-tool or a book about his hobby; $25 to $75 covers a meat thermometer, a daypack or a coffee grinder; $75 and up gets you headphones or an experience day together. Siblings can also pool their money for one bigger gift.
