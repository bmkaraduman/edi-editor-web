Knowing how to choose the perfect gift isn't about luck or a big budget. It's about asking the right questions in the right order, so you go from "I have no idea" to a short list of good options in a few minutes.

## Step 1: Who is the gift for?

Start with the person, not the shop. Two basics narrow things down more than anything else: who they are and what stage of life they're in.

- **Her or him**: plenty of gifts work for anyone, but jewelry, grooming kits and clothing usually depend on it.
- **Age**: a baby, a teenager, a young adult in a first apartment and a retired grandparent need completely different things. A great gift for a 16-year-old can feel odd for a 60-year-old, and the other way round.

Then think about your relationship. A partner can get something intimate and personal; a colleague should get something pleasant and neutral. The closer you are, the more personal you can go.

## Step 2: What's the occasion?

The occasion sets the tone and often the budget.

- **Birthdays** are about the person: their hobbies, their year, their quirks.
- **Christmas and holidays** often mean many gifts at once, so plan a budget per person.
- **Anniversaries and Valentine's Day** call for something romantic or shared.
- **Milestones** like a graduation, a new baby, a housewarming or a retirement are about the new chapter: help them start it well.
- **Thank-you and get-well gifts** should be small, kind and easy to receive.

## Step 3: What is their personality?

This is where a gift goes from fine to perfect. Think about how they spend a free Saturday.

- Do they read, cook, game, garden, travel, work out or make things?
- Are they a homebody who loves a cozy evening, or always out hiking?
- Is their phone full of pet photos, coffee art or concert videos?

Pick the two or three traits that describe them best and look for gifts where they overlap. A bookworm who loves tea? A reading pillow and a tea sampler. A traveler who loves photography? A travel journal and a compact tripod. The overlap is where the most personal ideas hide.

## Step 4: Set a budget before you browse

Decide on a number first, then shop. It stops you overspending on one person and underspending on another, and it makes choosing far easier.

- **Under $25**: small, thoughtful gifts, stocking stuffers, office exchanges.
- **$25 to $50**: the sweet spot for friends, siblings and most birthdays.
- **$50 to $100**: close family, partners and bigger occasions.
- **$100 and up**: milestones, group gifts or something they've wanted for a long time.

A modest gift that fits the person beats an expensive one that misses.

## Step 5: Choose a category, if you have one in mind

Sometimes you already know the kind of thing you want: jewelry, a book, something for the kitchen, a garden tool, a gift for their pet, an experience. Choosing a category at the end narrows the list to exactly that. If you don't have one in mind, skip this step and let the first four do the work; you might discover an idea you hadn't considered.

## Gift-giving mistakes to avoid

- **Buying for yourself.** If you love it but they never would, it's your gift, not theirs.
- **Guessing sizes.** Clothes and shoes are risky unless you know for sure; accessories are safer.
- **"Self-improvement" gifts** like a scale or a self-help book can land badly unless they asked.
- **Duplicating what they own.** Hobbyists often have the basics; look for accessories, consumables or upgrades.
- **Leaving it to the last minute.** Personalized and handmade gifts need time, and shipping can be slow around the holidays.
- **Forgetting the gift receipt.** It makes an exchange easy and painless for everyone.

## FAQ

### How do I find a gift for someone who has everything?

Focus on consumables and experiences rather than more things: good food, a tasting set, a class or an outing together. Our guide to [gifts for dad](blog:gifts-for-dad) has more ideas for hard-to-shop-for people.

### How much should I spend on a gift?

There's no fixed rule. Spend what feels comfortable for your relationship and the occasion, and keep it consistent across people in the same group, such as siblings or colleagues.

### Is a gift card a good gift?

It's a fine choice when you truly don't know their taste, or when they're saving for something specific. Make it more personal by choosing a shop that matches their hobby and adding a handwritten note.
